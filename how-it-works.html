The network and the wall socket

We build a network on which your data stays where it is. The question travels to it, the computation happens on the spot, and only the answer leaves. This page explains how that works and why it is needed, without assuming you know anything about the subject.

Three examples, not the full scope

They show the shape of what becomes possible. Freehold is a network on which many more kinds of question can run, and these three are here because they make the mechanism clear fastest.

Three banks

Together they establish whether a payment is suspicious, without any one of them seeing another's customer file. Today that question can only be answered by pooling the files, so mostly it is not asked at all.

A hospital and a research group

They reach an answer about a treatment while the patient records never leave the hospital. That is exactly where things go wrong today, because data that leaves once acquires a second and a third life at parties the patient has never seen.

A machine builder

It warns the owner that servicing is due without taking possession of that factory's production data. The owner keeps the figures, the builder gets the answer, and neither of them does it out of kindness.

One comparison runs through the whole page, the electricity grid. A hundred years ago the same thing happened there: a line was laid, one socket became the standard, and after that thousands of other people built appliances for it. We think data now stands at that point. At the end you will also find where the comparison breaks, because it does not hold everywhere.

ONE

The absent market for data

People have been talking about a market for data for twenty years. That market does not exist, and not because something is going wrong. It is because data does not have the property that makes a price come about.

In shortA price arises because there is too little of something. There is never too little of your data, because a copy costs nothing and cannot be told apart from the original. So a price never forms, and without a price there is no market but a distribution in which one side takes everything.

Every copy is as good as the original and costs almost nothing to make. So a shortage never arises, and neither does a price. That is not an opinion about today's market, it is a statement about what data is.

TWO

From data to answers

So we do not trade data. We make it possible for two parties to exchange an answer that leaves both of them better off. An answer is the outcome of one bounded question, computed on data that stays where it is.

In shortAn insurer does not need your medical file. It needs to know one thing. A shop does not need your purchase history. It needs to know whether this product suits you. In both cases the answer is a fraction of the file, and in both cases that answer is exactly what is required.

Why an answer does carry a price while data does not. It is made for one question from one requester, it is computed at the moment the question is put, and the next requester puts the question differently. A copy of an answer therefore loses its value, because that value hangs on a particular moment and a particular question. The scarcity here is not something we organise, it follows from what an answer is.

THREE

The direction of the question

This is the reversal, and it is the core of everything that follows. Today your data travels to the party that wants something from it. With us the question travels to your data, and only the answer travels back.

In shortCompare it with solar panels. You do not give away your roof and you do not give away an installation. You supply power to the grid and you get something back for it. The panels stay on your roof. It works the same way here: your data stays with you, and what goes to the network is the result.

What you get back is usually not money. Money is the simplest form and the rarest. The ordinary form is a better offer or a better condition, so the product that actually suits you or a more favourable term. The third form is safety, so a warning about your own installation, your own machine or your own health. That third one stands apart, because it cannot be resold and no second market therefore forms behind your back.

FOUR

The envelope around an answer

An answer without provenance is an assertion, and an assertion is worth nothing. So an answer never leaves us bare. A signed envelope goes around it, recording where it comes from and what it may be used for.

In shortThink of a driving licence. It proves you may drive without you having to recount your driving test. An envelope does the same, with two differences. It is made per occasion rather than reused for years, and it says what it is for. Whoever receives it keeps the envelope as evidence of having acted correctly, and has never seen the contents of your file.

There is no revocation channel, and that is a choice. On this time scale the systems exchange question and answer up to dozens of times a minute, so asking again costs almost nothing. So the recipient shows a valid envelope, instead of having to demonstrate that no revocation notice reached them.

FIVE

The connection point and the network

Up to here this was about one answer. This is about what arises when there are many connection points. The electricity grid never succeeded through the grid alone. It succeeded because one standard arrived and one socket, after which anyone was free to build an appliance for it.

In shortAt your home or in the business there is a connection point on the network, and a Hub runs on it. Compare the Hub with the fuse box: it hangs there, you do not notice it, and it determines what comes in. Together with the place where your data sits it forms what we call the trusted infrastructure, and the question comes in from outside that boundary. Every computation allowed to run there has first been tested and admitted and appears on a list of validated algorithms. If it is not on the list, it runs nowhere.
The device already exists
That box is not a drawing. It runs as a working prototype under the name Semek, it sits next to your router, and today it checks photos, videos and text without anything leaving your home. Aaltra, a Belgian technology company, is developing it in collaboration with Freehold, and it is the device the Hub will run on. See semek.ai.

The governance of register and tariff

Whoever determines which computation is admitted, and whoever determines which traffic runs at cost, exercises authority over everyone else building on the same network. That cannot sit with a party that sells applications there itself, and so it cannot sit with us alone. Both decisions belong to a board on which Freehold does not sit by itself, with published conditions and with a right of appeal against a refusal. Alongside Freehold Works there is a foundation for that purpose, and the arrangement only works under hard conditions: its own board without a majority of ours, its own funding, published admission conditions, an appeal route, and the ability to admit a competitor of ours and to refuse us if it comes to that.

For the same reason we write the agreement by which two systems find each other, and we publish it. Whoever represents one side cannot credibly run the place where both sides meet.

SIX

The break points of the comparison

The picture of the electricity grid carries far, and it does not carry everything. Below is where it stops.

The form of the return

Whoever supplies power is paid per kilowatt hour, pure quantity times tariff. With us what you get back is first of all the better offer itself. A model that promises you money for your data above all else is precisely the construction that has not worked these past twenty years.

The incomparability of answers

Two kilowatt hours are identical and therefore tradable on an exchange. Two answers never are. Pricing works differently here and no exchange will form.

The absence of a monopoly

A grid operator holds a regulated monopoly. We do not. To the question of who gave us this network, the answer is that the participants give it, per computation and in advance. They can also take it back.

The free alternative

Everyone is on the electricity grid because there is no alternative. For data that alternative does exist, namely the internet as it works today. That is at once the heaviest objection, because free is a strong competitor.

The run-up of electrification

In 1899 three per cent of American homes had electric light. It took about four decades after the first central station before half was reached, and in Great Britain five per cent of houses were wired in 1919. Gas there remained cheaper per unit of energy until at least 1930.

FURTHER

Further material

The six movements above are the whole story. Anyone who wants more will find three subjects below that follow directly from it. You do not need them to understand the rest.

The development layer

A network on which only the operator builds is not a utility but a product. So we open the network, and that immediately raises a question: how do you keep it safe once the computations are no longer yours.

One distinction sits underneath all of it. Composing a question is not the same as writing a new computation. The great majority of builders do not want a new computation. They want an existing, already admitted computation applied to their own counterparties, with their own threshold. The electricity grid has that same distinction and nobody finds it strange: putting a plug in a socket needs no certification, putting a new appliance on the market does.

What does this builder want to do

The hard part is that a builder must be able to develop without ever seeing the data. That takes three kinds of tooling: credible synthetic data per type of connection point, a local imitation that imposes the same limits on the builder's laptop as the real environment does, and a trial run on real points belonging to holders who explicitly volunteer for it, where only summarised diagnostics leave and never an answer value.

The question ceiling

One answer is bounded. But anyone allowed to put a hundred slightly different questions rebuilds the underlying file out of the answers. That is the principal line of attack on the whole mechanism, and care on our part is no defence against it once other people write the computations.

So there is a ceiling per holder, per recipient and per time window, on the number of answers and on what they give away together. That ceiling depletes, the holder can see it deplete, and it refills only on the holder's terms. At admission it is also established how much an answer gives away about the input, and that figure determines what one answer costs out of the ceiling.

There is a second reason as well, and it is commercial. As long as consent is a yes or a no, it cannot be priced. A ceiling that depletes is a quantity, and a quantity can be negotiated over.

That ceiling is a design choice when opening the network. What has been set down first is the envelope and the check on it.

The name and the patent application

Freehold is the name of what we build. A freehold is land held in full ownership, as against feudal tenure, where you hold land from a lord to whom you owe service and dues. Every data platform today works in that second way: you get access to a service and you pay for it in the one currency you do not get back. Freehold is the other arrangement. Someone who owns their land sells the harvest at market. They do not sell the land, and they do not let the market come and dig it up.
