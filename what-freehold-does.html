What Freehold does

A Hub at your door decides what comes in and what goes out. Your own files stay home.

Freehold runs the Freehold Insights Ecosystem. Freehold keeps every Hub up to date and keeps adding new things it can do for you.

The Hub

Picture a second box next to the box that brings the internet into your house.

You plug it in once and forget about it, like the fuse box under the stairs. It quietly minds what comes in and what goes out. That box is a Hub.

It exists today as Semek, a working prototype developed by Freehold's Products lead. In every browser in your home it spots photos, videos and text made by AI, and labels them, blurs them or turns them away. Nothing goes to a cloud. More at semek.ai.

The same Hub can run on your phone, in an office or in a factory. Only the place changes. The job stays the same.

The two directions

Think of the Hub as a door with somebody standing at it, and that somebody works for you.

You set the dial for both directions, and one of the settings is off. Off is allowed, and it always will be.

In

Coming in

The internet sends you a lot of rubbish. The Hub pushes the good things to the front. It looks at where something came from and whether a machine made it, and it follows your rules, whoever sent it.

Out

Going out

Today a company gets your raw information and still knows too little. The maker of your air conditioner sees the temperature, and misses that your window is open. With Freehold it sends its question to your Hub, which works out the real answer and sends back only that.

The sealed note

An insurer needs to know one thing, say whether you are fit to drive right now. Your medical records stay with you.

The answer travels inside something like a sealed note. Think of a driving licence, written fresh every single time.

Lots of Hubs together

One Hub in one house is handy. A few million of them, all speaking the same language, is something much bigger.

Think of plug sockets. Once everybody agreed on one shape, anyone could make a kettle that fits the wall. The people who ran the electricity network never made a single kettle. Freehold runs the ecosystem every Hub hangs on, and lets others build for it, AI models included.

Nothing runs on your Hub until it has been checked and put on a list. Whoever decides that list holds a lot of power, so a separate organisation decides, with its own board, rules anyone can read, and a way to complain if you are turned down.

The large AI models

Most questions your Hub answers itself, at home, in a moment. Every so often a question is genuinely hard, and one of the giant AI systems is better at it. Then the Hub sends as little as it possibly can: the paragraph that needs reading. Your name, the rest of the file and what you asked yesterday stay home.

The Hub is allowed to use a giant. It must never need one in order to think.

Four misunderstandings

What Freehold does not do, in four lines.

Data sales

Freehold does not sell your data. A copy costs nothing, so data never gets a proper price. An answer is made for one person, one question, one moment.

File access

Freehold does not read through your files. They stay put on every setting. What gets shared is one short answer.

Payment for data

Freehold does not pay you for your data. You get a better deal: the offer that suits you, or a warning about your own boiler or your own health.

The box

Freehold is not the box. The Hub decides, and today it runs on a box. What you buy into is the ecosystem.

The name and the patent

A farmer who owns a field shares the crop and keeps the field.

Freehold is an old English word for land you own outright. The way all of this works is the subject of European patent application 26202295.7, Improved authentication for processing requests, filed on 1 September 2026. A separate foundation watches over how it gets used.
